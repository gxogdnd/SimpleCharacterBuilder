# 3. Claiming your first issue

**You'll learn:** how to find a task that fits you and let others know you're on
it.

## Find an issue

Open the [Issues tab](https://github.com/gxogdnd/SimpleCharacterBuilder/issues).
Each issue is a unit of work. For your first one, filter by the
**`good first issue`** label — these are deliberately small and well-described.

A good issue tells you:

- **What** to do and **why** it matters,
- **Acceptance criteria** — how we'll know it's done,
- **Where to look** — the files you'll likely touch.

If anything is unclear, that's not your fault — comment and ask.

## Claim it

Leave a short comment so two people don't do the same work:

> I'd like to work on this one. 🙋

There's no need to wait for formal permission on a `good first issue` — claiming
it is mostly a courtesy to others.

## Understand "done" before you start

Re-read the acceptance criteria. Picture the finished change. If you can't yet,
ask a clarifying question on the issue. Five minutes of questions saves an hour
of guessing.

## A note on scope

Do **only** what the issue asks. Spotted something else to fix? Open a new
issue for it. Keeping changes small is the single biggest favour you can do your
reviewer (and your future self).
